Students of Indonesian politics learn early about *aliran*, the old streams that once divided voters between nationalist and Islamic parties. Many have since declared the cleavage dead, dissolved into patronage, personalities and money. Maps drawn at the level of neighborhoods and villages suggest it is still alive, if in new forms. Here are two.

## Two Jakartas

![Two Jakartas: PDI-P's north-west, PKS's south and east](/images/blog/old-cleavages-new-maps/jakarta-two-jakartas.jpg)

**Jakarta splits in two.** In 2019, PDI-P led in nearly every neighborhood (*kelurahan*) of the north and west. PKS's leads were concentrated in the south and the centre-east, where the two parties traded neighborhoods block by block.

**By 2024 the balance had tilted toward PKS.** It came first in two of Jakarta's three electoral districts: the centre-south (22.5% against PDI-P's 13.9%) and the east (21.9% against 16.2%), a district where PDI-P had led most neighborhoods in 2019. PDI-P held only the north-west (16.7% against 13.9%). The lower maps show that both parties' strength is gradual rather than sharp, but the gradient runs the same way: red to the north, orange to the south.

## The red on the santri coast

![Leading party in each village of Kudus, Jepara and Demak, 2019](/images/blog/old-cleavages-new-maps/jateng-ii-villages.jpg)

**Kudus, Jepara and Demak sit on Central Java's north coast,** the *pesisir*, home of the Demak mosque and the shrines of the *wali*, and long a heartland of traditionalist Islam. Here we would expect PKB, the party of the Nahdlatul Ulama community, to dominate.

**It does not.** In 2019 PKB led across much of western Demak and in pockets of Jepara. But PDI-P led in a large share of villages across all three districts, and NasDem, Golkar and Gerindra each held their own clusters. Even on the santri coast, the map is a patchwork, not a block of green.

**The two maps tell one story.** The old divide between nationalist and Islamic parties still shapes where parties win. But it now runs through neighborhoods and villages rather than across whole regions, and it is crossed everywhere by local networks and candidates. *Aliran* has not disappeared. It has fragmented.
