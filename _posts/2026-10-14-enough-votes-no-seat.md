I have been wondering how hard it really is for a newcomer from a small party to get into the DPR. Not in some remote district where nobody knows the candidates, but in the capital, where a fresh face with a following should have the best shot. So I took the official 2024 results and looked at Jakarta II.

Jakarta II is a slightly odd district. It covers Central and South Jakarta and, for reasons that make sense mainly to election administrators, every Indonesian who votes abroad. It elects seven members, and in 2024 it counted 2.54 million valid votes. The map below shows the capital's three districts. Jakarta II, in gold, is also the most expensive: a party needed 161,019 votes to win its last seat there, against about 130,000 in the other two.

Now the small parties. By "small" I mean the ten parties (marked † in the table) that did not reach 4 percent of the national vote, the threshold for getting any DPR seat at all. Between them they won 413,353 votes in Jakarta II, about one voter in six. The number of seats those votes bought: zero.

| Party | Votes in Jakarta II | Share | Seats |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| PKS | 483,058 | 19.0% | 2 |
| PDI-P | 368,762 | 14.5% | 1 |
| Gerindra | 329,827 | 13.0% | 1 |
| Golkar | 259,126 | 10.2% | 1 |
| PAN | 207,623 | 8.2% | 1 |
| PKB | 202,696 | 8.0% | 1 |
| **PSI †** | **183,836** | **7.2%** | **0** |
| Demokrat | 148,440 | 5.8% | 0 |
| NasDem | 127,864 | 5.0% | 0 |
| 9 other small parties † | 229,517 | 9.0% | 0 |
| **Total** | **2,540,749** | | **7** |

Look at PSI in the table. It won 183,836 votes, more than the price of a seat. Had PSI passed the national threshold, its total would have ranked seventh in Jakarta II and taken the last seat, which went instead to PKS's second candidate. Ade Armando, PSI's top candidate with 54,199 votes, would be sitting in Senayan today. Jakarta's voters did their part; the rest of the country did not.

Ade Armando was hardly a typical newcomer, of course. Like Perindo's Liliana Tanoesoedibjo, he arrived with a famous name. The other small-party candidates had much less to show. The ten small parties fielded 69 candidates in Jakarta II. Half of them won fewer than 969 votes, roughly an extended family and a few loyal neighbours. Only five passed 10,000.

So can a newcomer win at all? Yes, but on a big party's list. The table below compares the seven winners with the three best small-party candidates. Three winners were first-timers: Uya Kuya, the TV presenter (PAN, 81,463 votes), the lawyer Abraham Sridjaja (Golkar, 76,945) and the singer Once Mekel (PDI-P, 60,623). Once Mekel finished only about 6,400 votes ahead of Ade Armando. The difference between Senayan and going home was the party logo next to the name.

| Candidate | Party | Votes | Worth | Result |
|---|---|--:|--:|---|
| M. Hidayat Nur Wahid | PKS | 227,974 | 1.42 | Re-elected |
| Himmatul Aliyah | Gerindra | 94,543 | 0.59 | Re-elected |
| Uya Kuya | PAN | 81,463 | 0.51 | First term |
| Abraham Sridjaja | Golkar | 76,945 | 0.48 | First term |
| Ida Fauziyah | PKB | 76,092 | 0.47 | Returned (ex-minister) |
| Kurniasih Mufidayati | PKS | 62,755 | 0.39 | Re-elected |
| Once Mekel | PDI-P | 60,623 | 0.38 | First term |
| Ade Armando | PSI | 54,199 | 0.34 | Lost (party below 4%) |
| Sintawati | PPP | 46,920 | 0.29 | Lost (party below 4%) |
| Liliana T. Tanoesoedibjo | Perindo | 38,257 | 0.24 | Lost (party below 4%) |

The "worth" column divides each candidate's votes by the seat price. Only Hidayat Nur Wahid of PKS, with 227,974 votes, won enough on his own to fill a seat. Everyone else, newcomer or veteran, needed the party's pooled votes to get over the line. That is how our open lists work: seats go to parties first, and only then to the best-polling candidates inside each party.

So the answer to the title is: rarely, and mostly for reasons the candidate cannot control. A small-party newcomer in Jakarta II needs the party to clear 4 percent nationwide and then to gather about 161,000 votes in the district before personal popularity even enters the picture. If a young idealist in Jakarta asked me for advice, I would suggest, a little cynically, a big party's list. Or a great deal of patience.
