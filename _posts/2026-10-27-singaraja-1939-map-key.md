*–all twenty-nine labels, in today's spelling.*

<p class="post-tags"><span class="tag">#history</span> <span class="tag">#Bali</span></p>

In my [last post](/blog/wijk-kampoeng-bandjar/) I used about a dozen of the labels on C. J. Grader's 1939 sketch of Singaraja. The map has twenty-nine. This post is the full key, for anyone from Singaraja who wants to find their own neighbourhood on it, and for anyone who simply likes old maps. The red numbers are mine; the [unmarked original](/images/blog/wijk-kampoeng-bandjar/desa-boeleleng-1939-original.jpg){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} is here.

First, three things to know before you start.

**Which way is up.** There is no north arrow, but the harbour is at the top, so north is roughly up. The hills lie south, at the bottom. That means *kaja*, towards the mountain, is south here, and *kelod*, towards the sea, is north.

**The spelling.** It is Dutch-era: *oe* is u, *dj* is j, *tj* is c. So *Boelèlèng* is Buleleng, *Bandjar Djawa* is Banjar Jawa, and *Poera* is Pura.

**The scale.** The title says 1:20,000, so a centimetre on Grader's drawing was 200 metres on the ground. The plate was shrunk for printing, though, so do not measure distances off the copy you see here.

Now the labels. Page numbers refer to Grader's article.

| # | On the map | In today's terms | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Chineesche wijk* | Chinese quarter | East bank of the river mouth, by the harbour: the trading district. |
| 2 | *Poera Segara* | Pura Segara, the sea temple | One of the three village temples that all the *banjar*s worship (p. 340). |
| 3 | *Mohammadaansche begraafplaats* | Muslim cemetery | Beside the harbour, among the Muslim quarters. |
| 4 | *weg naar Sangsit* | Road to Sangsit | Heading east. Sangsit's ornate temple style opens Grader's article (p. 330). |
| 5 | *Arabische wijk* | Arab quarter | Harbour district. |
| 6 | *Kampoeng Tinggi* | Kampung Tinggi | East of the river. Where I grew up. |
| 7 | *Boegineesche wijk* | Buginese quarter | On the spit west of the harbour; Bugis seafarers and traders. |
| 8 | *Kampoeng Kadjanan* | Kampung Kajanan | |
| 9 | *Kampoeng Bali* | Kampung Bali | A *kampoeng*, not a *banjar*. See the [last post](/blog/wijk-kampoeng-bandjar/). |
| 10 | ⚓ | Harbour | Buleleng's old roadstead and port. |
| 11 | *Bandjar Djawa* | Banjar Jawa | |
| 12 | *Bandjar Tengah* | Banjar Tengah, "middle ward" | |
| 13 | *Bandjar Pegoejangan* | Banjar Peguyangan | Probably right, but the lettering is faint. |
| 14 | *weg naar Pengastoelan* | Road to Pengastulan | Heading west, towards the newer settlements (p. 330). |
| 15 | *Sandjar Polak* | Banjar Petak, or Polak | "Sandjar" is the draughtsman's slip for *Bandjar*. The name itself is hard to read. |
| 16 | *Sema* | Burial and cremation ground | Next to the Pura Dalem, as usual. |
| 17 | *Poera Dalem* | Pura Dalem, temple of the dead | Second of the three village temples. |
| 18 | *Bandjar Banjoemala* | Banjar Banyumala | A detached settlement by the western river. Banyumala is also a *subak* that shares the village temples (p. 340). |
| 19 | *Bandjar Penataran* | Banjar Penataran | |
| 20 | *Bandjar Dangin Peken* | Banjar Dangin Peken, "east of the market" | |
| 21 | *Bandjar Liligoendi* | Banjar Liligundi | |
| 22 | *Sema* | Burial and cremation ground | A second one, in the west, by Banyumala. |
| 23 | *Bandjar Tegal* | Banjar Tegal | The subject of Grader's article. Its temple, the Pura Pamajoen, is not marked; Grader puts it near the middle of the *banjar*, on the mountain side (p. 342), so on the southern edge of this block. |
| 24 | *Poera Desa* | Pura Desa, the village temple | Third of the three village temples. It stands at the crossroads where the purification rite before Nyepi is held in alternate years (p. 361). |
| 25 | *Bandjar Paketan* | Banjar Paketan | |
| 26 | *weg naar Beratan* | Road to Beratan | Heading south, to the smiths' village, whose temples Grader found honouring Muslim, Madurese and Chinese gods (pp. 357–359). |
| 27 | *Grens bebouwde kom* | Boundary of the built-up area | The thick toothed line. A *banjar*'s territory stops here: people in field huts outside it are not automatically members (p. 340). |
| 28 | *Europeesche woonwijk* | European residential quarter | The hatched strip down the main road. |
| 29 | *Hoofdwegen* | Main roads | Double lines with arrows. |

Two rivers wind through the map as thin double lines, unlabelled. The eastern one runs down to the harbour and is presumably the Tukad Buleleng. The western one curls past Banyumala and Tegal, which fits the Tukad Banyumala; Grader says holy water for Banjar Tegal's temple festival is fetched from a spring in its ravine (p. 345).

A last caution. The scan I worked from is about 100 dpi, so 13 is a probable reading and 15 a guess, and the map shows the town of the late 1930s. Many of these names are still kelurahan today, but the boundaries have moved. If you find your own *banjar* here and think I have it wrong, I would be glad to hear it.
