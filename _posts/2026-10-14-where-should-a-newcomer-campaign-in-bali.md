*–north, east, and inside your own party.*

<p class="post-tags"><span class="tag">#data</span> <span class="tag">#elections</span> <span class="tag">#Bali</span></p>

Suppose you want to sit in the DPR for Bali, and you are not from PDI-P. Where do you spend your campaign money? The lazy answer is "join PDI-P". Fair enough, but let us assume our candidate is stubborn, and look at the numbers.

First, the scale of the problem. Bali is one electoral district with nine seats. In 2024 PDI-P took 52.5 percent of the vote and five of the seats. The other four went to Golkar, Gerindra, Demokrat and NasDem, one each. A seat cost about 143,000 votes. So there is room outside PDI-P, but only about four chairs of it.

Now the geography. We have no public 2024 DPR result below the province, so I went back to 2019, when KPU's real count gives every village. The map below shades each kecamatan by the share of the vote that did not go to PDI-P. The pattern is old and stubborn: the pale belt of Tabanan, Badung and Gianyar is PDI-P's fortress, wet-rice country where its rivals struggle to reach a third of the vote. The dark edges, the north coast of Buleleng, Karangasem in the east and Nusa Penida, are where the rivals live.

Shares, though, can mislead. Klungkung is very competitive but small; a campaign needs votes, not percentages. The table below counts them.

| Kabupaten/kota | Votes not for PDI-P, 2019 | Share of local vote | Share of all rival votes | Council seats outside PDI-P, 2024 |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Buleleng | 218,056 | 57% | 21% | 6 of 12 |
| Karangasem | 179,058 | 69% | 17% | 4 of 7 |
| Denpasar | 162,125 | 48% | 16% | 4 of 8 |
| Badung | 106,832 | 36% | 10% | 2 of 6 |
| Gianyar | 88,326 | 29% | 8% | 1 of 6 |
| Jembrana | 84,938 | 53% | 8% | 2 of 4 |
| Tabanan | 76,667 | 26% | 7% | 2 of 6 |
| Klungkung | 68,941 | 65% | 7% | 1 of 3 |
| Bangli | 58,458 | 42% | 6% | 1 of 3 |
| **Bali** | **1,043,401** | **46%** | **100%** | **23 of 55** |

Three places, Buleleng, Karangasem and Denpasar, held more than half of all the votes PDI-P's rivals won in Bali in 2019. Tabanan, Gianyar and Badung together, despite holding almost 40 percent of the island's voters, gave them only about a quarter. The last column is a check from 2024: in the provincial council, the parties outside PDI-P won half the seats in Buleleng and Denpasar and four of seven in Karangasem, but one of six in Gianyar. Five years on, the map still holds.

There is a further wrinkle. In every kabupaten Golkar is the biggest of PDI-P's rivals, holding 35 to 45 percent of the non-PDI-P vote in Buleleng and Karangasem. In Denpasar it holds only 24 percent, and the rest is scattered across PSI, NasDem, Gerindra, Demokrat and the Islamic parties. The north and east are rich in rival votes, but somebody already owns many of them. The city is smaller, but more of it is up for grabs. For a newcomer without an old machine, Denpasar may be the friendlier market.

And then comes the part nobody puts on campaign posters. Your real opponent is probably not PDI-P. It is the person above you on your own party's list.

| Candidate | Party | Party votes | List no. | Votes | Result |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| Gde Sumarjaya Linggih | Golkar | 333,521 | 2 | 100,747 | Won |
| I Nyoman Sugawa Korry | Golkar | 333,521 | 1 | 82,681 | Runner-up in party |
| I Dewa Gde Agung Widiarsana | Gerindra | 295,313 | 1 | 80,658 | Won |
| Gede Ngurah Ambara Putra | Gerindra | 295,313 | 2 | 38,168 | Runner-up in party |
| Tutik Kusuma Wardhani | Demokrat | 174,476 | 9 | 54,713 | Won |
| Putu Supadma Rudana | Demokrat | 174,476 | 2 | 49,243 | Runner-up in party |
| I Nengah Senantara | NasDem | 147,678 | 1 | 51,563 | Won |
| Masrur Makmur | NasDem | 147,678 | 6 | 39,504 | Runner-up in party |

Each of these parties won exactly one seat, so only its top vote-getter went to Jakarta. The bar was somewhere between 50,000 and 100,000 personal votes. In Demokrat, Tutik Kusuma Wardhani, number nine on the list (and herself a former member), beat the sitting member, Putu Supadma Rudana, number two, by about 5,500 votes. List position, in other words, is decoration. And the party has to win a seat in the first place. The smallest party that did, NasDem, had about 148,000 votes across Bali; PSI, with 61,000, was nowhere near.

What about home? Our village data show that a party does about five points better in the village where its DPR candidate lives. That sounds nice until you remember that a typical Balinese village casts about 2,500 votes. Home is worth a hundred-odd votes and a warm welcome at the banjar. The other 50,000 have to come from somewhere else.

So my advice to our stubborn candidate: pick a party big enough to win a seat at all, campaign north and east if your party already has roots there, and in Denpasar if it does not, and spend at least as much energy worrying about your list-mates as about PDI-P. Or, of course, join PDI-P.
